Why now

Someone told your mission to move. The provider gives you a landing zone. It does not give you your picture.

A data-center closure, an enterprise mandate, a task order. The award has its own clock, and the landing zone’s own authorization behind it runs twenty-four to twenty-eight weeks in the provider’s work stream. Most of the work that decides whether your applications can use that landing zone does not depend on the letter arriving, and every week done in parallel is a week not spent serialising afterward. This is the shape of a two-environment engagement as it actually runs; one environment alone runs the same shape.

Two clocks, and they overlap. Six weeks is Foundations: your people, your evidence, the boundary drawn and categorized, the signed plan, the fifteen deliverables. It is fixed-fee and it is what most engagements buy first. Application Dependency Mapping is the second clock and a separate engagement — twelve weeks minimum, sixteen for core applications. Application observation happens inside it, not inside the six weeks. They overlap; they do not add.

Sixteen weeks of work; week 20 on the calendar. Sixteen weeks covers the whole arc across multiple environments — Foundations, the mapping, and the segmentation pre-plan. The record closes at week 20 because two of those weeks are the data call before the clock starts, and the second environment stands up two weeks behind the first. The figure above draws the calendar; the sixteen is the work inside it.

And the landing zone’s own authorization is not on this clock. That is the provider’s work stream, not ours, and it runs twenty-four to twenty-eight weeks. The plan does not wait for it. That is the whole point of starting now: the record is ready and signed well before the platform it describes is authorized to receive anything.

The observation window is set by the estate, not by us. A month is the minimum that captures how compute actually varies across a cycle; a shorter look reads one week’s noise as the pattern.
