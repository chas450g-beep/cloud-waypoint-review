OBSERVE BEFORE YOU REDESIGN

Observed does not mean approved.

A strong perimeter can still leave overly broad access between internal systems. Telemetry shows communications visible during the observation period—not which connections are necessary or permitted. We combine that evidence with application-owner knowledge and existing policy to identify required access, unnecessary exposure, and gaps needing further review.

CONVERGED OUTPUTS

Evidence that travels forward.

01

Affinity groups

Applications and services considered together for migration. Shared dependencies do not automatically require a shared migration wave or security boundary.

02

Migration waves

Sequenced movement based on coupling, readiness, mission priority, risk, and target dependencies.

03

Target data flows

Proposed future connections based on validated mission need, data sensitivity, identity, and access requirements—not a copy of existing traffic.

04

Communities of interest

Candidate groupings based on mission purpose and access needs. Membership does not grant unrestricted communication between members.

05

Workload ATO inputs

Application dependencies, proposed flows, control inheritance, risks, and ownership evidence for the applicable system or application authorization process.

06

First-launch conditions

The services, decisions, remediations, and accountable owners needed before a workload moves.

ZERO TRUST & MICROSEGMENTATION

Validate the need. Approve the policy. Test the controls.

Mapping informs security design; it does not grant access. Designated owners approve policies and exceptions. Implementation teams then deploy and test controls, with monitoring and change ownership defined. Microsegmentation is one possible enforcement capability—not the whole of Zero Trust.
