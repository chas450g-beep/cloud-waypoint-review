The engagement method

What you bring. What we do. What moves forward.

Begin with existing material. Fill the gaps together. Carry one consistent account of the mission into planning, security review and transition.

  1. 01 Establish the starting point

    We ask for

    Application and asset inventories, current architecture, mission priorities and existing security or authorization documents.

    We work through

    Scope, information needs, current-state gaps and the officials who decide. Confirm the requirements and the boundary to examine.

    You take forward

    Documented mission context, a proposed boundary, an evidence position and named questions to resolve in the midpoint snapshot.

  2. 02 Connect the design and the responsibilities

    We ask for

    Available application communication data, hosting designs, provider security evidence, service agreements and operating procedures.

    We work through

    Application dependencies and modernization options; shared services and inherited controls; handoffs among mission, cloud foundation and cyber teams.

    You take forward

    Foundation design, application/dependency context, draft security plan and concept of operations, responsibility mapping and corrective actions.

  3. 03 Sequence the transition and hand over

    We ask for

    Delivery priorities, change windows, receiving-team constraints and review of the proposed design, documents and open actions.

    We work through

    Application modernization pathways, dependencies, transition sequence and acceptance responsibilities. Identify the implementation and assessment work still needed.

    You take forward

    The transformation plan, road ahead and executive brief, alongside draft authorization inputs and a closeout record with owners and outstanding work.

Inputs are tailored to the engagement and reviewed in the agreed client environment. Protected evidence stays there. Package content and acceptance follow the statement of work.

Choose the applicable review path

The method travels. The requirements vary.

Identify the governing authority and review criteria at the start. Use the provider’s existing security work where it applies, and make your organization’s responsibilities explicit.

DoD missions · RMF and the mission authorization

Align preparation to the applicable DoD and component instructions, mission information and proposed hosting boundary. Confirm inherited controls, mission-owned controls and the evidence the authorizing chain needs.

A DoD provisional authorization for a cloud service offering and the mission system’s Authorization to Operate (ATO) address different scopes. Any Interim Authorization to Test (IATT) has its own authorized conditions and limits.

Read the detailed RMF lifecycle guide ↗
Federal civilian · Agency RMF and FedRAMP evidence

Prepare for the agency’s system authorization. Where FedRAMP applies, review the cloud offering’s security package, what can be inherited and the configuration, integration and operating controls the agency retains.

FedRAMP addresses the provider’s cloud service offering. The agency still makes the risk decision for its own system. A provider pursuing a new FedRAMP qualification follows a separate provider assessment process.

FedRAMP guidance on agency use and shared responsibility ↗
State and local government · Jurisdiction and service requirements

Confirm the jurisdiction’s policies, data obligations, procurement conditions and adopted security frameworks. Connect application and transition planning to those review criteria and to provider/customer responsibilities.

NIST frameworks can support that work. Federal RMF or FedRAMP requirements apply only where the relevant authority, agreement or use case calls for them.

NIST on using the Cybersecurity Framework across sectors ↗
Education · Institutional security and privacy review

Start with institutional governance, the information involved and service-provider obligations. Include student privacy requirements such as FERPA where applicable, alongside security, procurement and operational review.

The same preparation method can connect the cloud move to those obligations. A federal ATO or FedRAMP path is not a universal education requirement.

Modernize where the mission must operate.

The right path may include an existing enterprise service, a cloud environment or modernization in place. Connectivity and operating responsibility belong in the design from the start.

Understand evidence and progress

Distinguish a drafted document from an operating practice. Track confirmed evidence, unresolved gaps and responsible owners. Compare progress only when baseline and closeout records use compatible scope and methods.

Preparing more complete documents can improve readiness without establishing operational maturity or an authorization outcome.
