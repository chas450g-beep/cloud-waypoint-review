Concept of operations

1 · Mission & operating context SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Foundations decision record
Mission and operating context USAF MC² Directorate (fictional) is establishing a general support system under USAF enterprise cloud task order (fictional), task order TO-MC2-2026-031 (fictional), over 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30. The awarded environment is AWS GovCloud (US) — USAF enterprise IL5 mission-cloud landing zone (fictional), operating at IL5 in GovCloud (US-West), dual-AZ, beside the internal shared platform the directorate already operates. This document describes how the mission intends to operate both under one boundary and one cadence, not how either platform is built. What the mission is trying to achieve: By the end of the engagement, the MC² Directorate holds a signed concept of operations for a governed internal shared platform beside the enterprise IL5 mission cloud, its twenty systems dispositioned with named owners, the first three IL5 candidates through the acceptance review, and a cadence that has already run once. The mission continues to deliver its existing services throughout this transition: maintenance scheduling, logistics readiness, installation work orders and training registration do not pause. Nothing in this concept of operations authorises a pause in current operations, and where a choice arises between transition progress and current service, current service prevails unless the Director decides otherwise in writing. Information handled by this system: Mission planning archives, maintenance and logistics readiness data, installation work orders, training records, deployment status (CUI). The impact determination and the basis retained for it are recorded in the foundation receipt. Constraints the mission treats as fixed: the base data-center lease ends with the option year; the enterprise migration order requires a disposition for every MC² system by the second quarter; the cross-domain gateway's boundary does not change. Constraints the mission is willing to revisit: the wave order after wave zero, and whether the hybrid cohort's second half moves in the option year. [SRC-1] [SRC-16]

2 · Operating environment & boundary SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: GSS foundation receipt · get-vs-own sheet
Operating environment and boundary The system boundary comprises the MC² tenancy operated for the directorate by USAF enterprise IL5 mission-cloud operator (fictional) within the enterprise IL5 zone, together with the internal shared platform the platform squad operates on-site, the services the mission consumes from the zone, and the crossings recorded in Annex C. Inside: the tenancy accounts (one per IL5 candidate and one shared non-production account), the internal platform's compute, delivery toolchain, monitoring and CMDB, and the mission workloads on both. Outside: the zone's own platform and inspection stack (inherited, the operator's), the enterprise directory beyond the federation trust, the cross-domain transfer gateway and the cyber defense analytics under their own specialized boundaries, the enterprise data warehouse as a system of record reached by agreement, and the base network beyond the cloud access point. Workloads permitted outside the primary cloud: the seven internal-platform systems by decision of the operating-model gate, governed by the platform product owner; the seven retained systems by specialized-boundary or system-of-record decision, governed by the ISSM and the data steward. Each exception is recorded with its governor in the foundation receipt and reviewed at the governance forum. [SRC-3] [SRC-4] [SRC-14]

3 · Operating model (build / contract / join) SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Team design · seam catalog · RACI
Operating model The mission operates on a join basis for the enterprise zone and a build basis for the internal shared platform. The zone already exists with an authorized boundary; joining it inherits the security stack and shared services and leaves the directorate to run its own workloads. The internal platform already exists with a squad, a toolchain and a CMDB; keeping it under the same concept of operations avoids a second operating model for the seven systems that stay. Building a separate MC² zone was costed at two quarters and a second authorization; contracting the platform out would have removed the day-two team the arc needs. The provider estate is the enterprise zone (primary), the internal platform (secondary) and the base data-center estate (exiting). Two teams meet in this operation and they are organised differently on purpose. The cloud team is the platform squad: seven engineers, breadth over depth, a named product owner, infrastructure as code reviewed in pull requests, on-call rotating weekly. The enterprise team is the base communications squadron and the enterprise operator: towers by discipline, a weekly change board, emergency changes by the duty officer. Where they meet routinely, by design rather than at incidents: interconnect changes (designed jointly), identity federation administration (the enterprise owns the source), patch baselines on enterprise-built images (the enterprise builds, the squad runs), and evidence retrieval across two ticket systems. Responsibility for each activity is recorded in Annex A across the named roles: MC² IT Directorate (system owner), MC² platform squad, base communications squadron, enterprise mission-cloud operator, and the ISSM. Each activity records whether it stays with the operator or migrates to the mission over time, so the operating model has a direction and not only a present state. Functions the mission does not intend to staff itself are named in Annex A with the party that covers them: boundary inspection, the identity source, physical and environmental protection, and cross-domain transfer. [SRC-7] [SRC-13]

4 · Service delivery SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Catalog entries · interconnect specs
Service delivery The mission offers its own users six services, each with an owner, its consumers, the interface through which it is requested, the level it is held to and the evidence its operation produces: the Logistics Readiness Portal, the Installation Work Order Service, the Training Registration Service, the Mission Planning Archive service, the deployment status dashboard, and the shared platform's identity and delivery brokerage. Annex B carries the entries and their design records; Annex C carries every crossing they depend on. What the mission promises its users: business-hours availability of 99.5 percent for the mission services and 99.9 percent for the platform's brokerage, support from 0600 to 1800 local on duty days with on-call escalation for mission-essential services, and a four-hour recovery time objective with a one-hour recovery point for the portal. Levels are held to the continuity plan's outage tolerances; where a service's level and the plan disagree, the plan governs and the entry is corrected. Requests are raised through the ITSM tool; fulfilment times are published with the catalogue. [SRC-9] [SRC-15]

5 · Operating cadence SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: ConMon & POA&M workflow (GSS-JSP-05)
Operating cadence The operation runs on the cadence in Annex D: automated scan review daily (the operator for the zone, the squad for the platform), continuous-monitoring review weekly (the ISSM with the cyber cell), plan of action and milestones review fortnightly (the ISSM, the AO on escalation), effectiveness and recurrence review monthly (the IT chief), and the walk-the-floor rehearsal quarterly (both teams). Each activity has an owner and a last-run date; the cadence ran once before this document was signed. The severity taxonomy in force is Critical fifteen days, High thirty, Moderate ninety, Low one hundred and eighty; risk acceptance above Moderate is the AO's on the ISSM's recommendation. The cadence activity that would go unnoticed if it quietly stopped is the effectiveness review; its record is therefore a standing item at the governance forum. The continuous-monitoring split between operator, platform squad, mission and cyber cell is recorded in the GSS exit receipt and printed in Annex D. [SRC-17] [SRC-23]

6 · Application onboarding SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Onboarding / acceptance review
Application onboarding An application enters either environment only through the acceptance review in Annex E: eleven criteria across built as specified, behaves as intended, and performs in the real process, seven of them blocking. The system owner is the accepting authority; the ISSM may refuse on findings position or evidence location; the platform product owner may refuse on reproducibility or monitoring. A refusal is recorded with its grounds and the application returns to the queue when the grounds are met; an application that fails twice goes to the governance forum with the owner present. Wave zero is the Logistics Readiness Portal alone, accepted with its rollback position recorded; the work-order service and training registration follow through the same review as wave one. What a candidate needs that the landing zone does not provide is recorded on its queue entry and becomes an Annex B or Annex C change before acceptance. [SRC-14] [SRC-22]

7 · Evidence & records SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Evidence-born-at-build · ticket bridge
Evidence and records Evidence this operation produces comes to rest in the evidence store named in Annex F: scan results daily, access review attestations quarterly, change records per change, restore records per rehearsal, cadence records per run, each with a retention period and an owner who can retrieve it. The system of record for changes is the ITSM tool; the rule linking evidence to work is that every artifact names the ticket that produced it and every closing ticket names where its evidence rests. What exists the morning someone asks, without anyone preparing it: the last scan result, the last access review, the open findings with their clocks, the last cadence run and the last rehearsal record, all retrievable by the ISSM from the store. Logs the operator holds are read weekly by the cyber cell on the operator's digest; the record of that reading is the cadence entry. Nothing in the store is a screenshot where an export exists. [SRC-23] [SRC-17]

8 · Readiness & sustainment SIGNED

ANNEX INSTRUMENT: Walk-the-floor rehearsal standard
Readiness and sustainment The operation is rehearsed to the standard in Annex G: six scenarios, each run once before signature, with actions raised where the documented behaviour did not match the observed. The rehearsal is walked with both teams in the room; a rehearsal without the operator's on-call present proves nothing about the boundary. When the people who wrote this document move on, it survives in the record: the annexes are instruments the cadence fills, not prose the authors carry; the retainer checkpoints read the record against the last one so drift is a fact, not an impression. What would tell the directorate, six months in, that the operating model chosen was the wrong one: the platform squad absorbing the towers' work without the trajectories moving, the change board still holding pipeline releases, or a restore that fails twice. Each is a trigger on the risk register with the Director as owner. Sustainment continues on the Silver retainer: two checkpoints a month for the first quarter, reviewed at the quarter's end. [SRC-7]

This annex records who is responsible for each operating activity, who is accountable for its outcome, and whether that responsibility stays with USAF enterprise IL5 mission-cloud operator (fictional) or migrates to USAF MC² Directorate (fictional) over time.
